About

Everyone arrested deserves to know restorative justice is on the table, not just whoever thinks to ask.

I'm Dean Mustaphalli. Before any of this, I spent years in finance, working at a top-tier Wall Street firm and later running my own investment fund. That career ended with a felony conviction, and I served time in New York State prison. While I was incarcerated, I ran a transition program, helping other men get through their programs, prepare for parole board hearings, and work on their appeals in the law library. That work changed my outlook, from Wall Street to serving people caught in a carceral system that is genuinely broken. I won my own parole board appeal and came home almost three years ago. Everything on this site comes from that experience: not a law degree, but years spent on the inside of a system most people only ever read about.

A young man once sat down next to me and asked how he could reduce his prison time. As I listened, the pattern was painfully familiar: a deal had been pushed on him before he understood what else was on the table, and now he had almost no recourse but to serve the sentence he'd already agreed to. Nobody had ever mentioned a diversion program existed. That conversation, and the ones just like it, is why this site exists.

Case Analytica exists because of one specific, repeated failure: restorative justice and diversion programs are real, they're running in New York courts right now, and almost nobody facing a case gets told about them unless they happen to ask. Public defenders carry heavy caseloads. Even private attorneys can move fast and skip past it. The result is that people plead into convictions that didn't have to happen, simply because nobody raised the option in time.

This channel and site exist to close that gap, so that knowing about restorative justice doesn't depend on luck, and so nobody has to just trust that their lawyer will remember to bring it up. The videos and articles also cover the rest of the process (bail, discovery, the Clean Slate Act, parole hearings), but restorative justice comes first, because it's the one piece of information that changes what's actually possible in a case, and it's the one most likely to get missed.

What this is not

Case Analytica does not provide legal advice or legal representation, and nothing here should be read as instructions for a specific case. If you need representation, we can help you find and connect with a licensed attorney or the right local resource.

How the free intake call works

A 20–30 minute conversation to understand where a case actually stands: what stage it's at, what the realistic timeline looks like, and what to ask for next. If it's a legal question that needs an attorney, we'll help point you to one. If it's a question about the process, reentry support, or a restorative justice option, we'll walk through what's actually available in your county.

If you want to come to that call organized, fill in the case worksheet first. It works on a phone, nothing you type is sent anywhere, and you can print it to bring with you.
