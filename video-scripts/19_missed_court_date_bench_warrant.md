# 19: What Happens If You Miss a Court Date in New York?

**Format:** Short · ~80 sec | **Pillar:** System | **Slot:** Week 7 (new batch, unscheduled)

## Script

**Hook (0:00)**
You missed a court date. Before you panic, there is one number that actually matters here, and it is not how many hours have passed. It is thirty days.

**Body (0:10)**
A missed date lets a judge issue a bench warrant under Criminal Procedure Law 530.70. That warrant can be executed anywhere in the state, and it does not mean you have been convicted of anything. It means the court now has a tool to bring you back in. Separately, New York's bail jumping laws, Penal Law 215.55 through 215.57, only turn a missed date into its own extra charge if you do not appear personally, voluntarily, within thirty days of the date you missed. That thirty days is written into the statute itself. It is not a grace period a judge is handing you out of kindness, and a warrant can still be active and executed inside that window. What it protects you from is the separate bail jumping charge, nothing else.

**Self-advocacy line (0:55)**
Do not wait to be told what is happening. Ask your attorney directly: "Has a bench warrant actually been issued on my case, and are we inside or outside the thirty day window for a bail jumping charge?" Get a straight answer before you decide anything, including whether to turn yourself in today or tomorrow.

**CTA (1:15)**
This is general information, not legal advice for your specific case. Link in bio if you have missed a date and need to understand what is actually active on your case right now.
