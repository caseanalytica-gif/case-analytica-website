# 16: Can You Expunge a Record in New York? What Sealing Under CPL 160.59 Really Does

**Format:** Short · ~75 sec | **Pillar:** Access to Justice | **Slot:** Week 5 (new batch, unscheduled)

## Script

**Hook (0:00)**
People search "how to expunge my record in New York" every single day. New York doesn't expunge anything. What it has instead is narrower, and almost nobody finds out it exists until years after they could have used it.

**Body (0:08)**
Expungement means the conviction is erased, like it never happened. New York doesn't do that. What New York offers is sealing, under Criminal Procedure Law 160.59, and it's not the same as the Clean Slate Act, which seals records automatically with no paperwork. This one you have to file for yourself. To qualify, it has to be at least ten years since your last sentencing or release, whichever came later, you can have no more than two eligible convictions with no more than one felony, and certain offenses, including sex offenses and violent felonies, are permanently excluded. Even then, a judge still weighs it: how much time has passed, what you've done since, whether the DA objects. This is discretionary relief, not automatic.

**CTA (0:55)**
Here's the part that surprises people most: the law says no prosecutor can ever make you waive your right to apply for this as part of a plea deal, any waiver like that is legally void. Ask directly: "do any of my old convictions qualify for sealing under 160.59, and what do I need to file?" This is general information, not legal advice for your specific record; link in bio if you or someone you love needs help figuring out what applies.
