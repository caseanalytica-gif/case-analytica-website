# 16: How Long Can NY Take You to Trial? CPL 30.30 Explained

**Format:** Short · ~75 sec | **Pillar:** Rights & Process | **Slot:** Week 5 (new batch, unscheduled)

## Script

**Hook (0:00)**
People ask me this constantly: it's been months, sometimes over a year, and their case still hasn't moved. Is there a law that forces this along? There is. Almost nobody explains what it actually measures.

**Body (0:08)**
It's Criminal Procedure Law 30.30, New York's speedy trial statute. It does not promise you a trial by a certain date. It sets a deadline for when prosecutors have to say they're ready for trial: six months for a felony, ninety days for a higher misdemeanor, sixty days for a lower one, thirty days for a violation. Miss that window without a valid excuse, and the case can be dismissed. Here's the catch: huge categories of time get excluded from that clock. Motions, competency proceedings, delays the defense agreed to. A case that's been open fourteen months on the calendar might have a legal 30.30 clock of far less than that.

**Self-advocacy line (0:50)**
Nobody adds this up for you automatically. It has to be calculated, and checked against the court file, every time. Ask your attorney directly: "What is my actual 30.30 date right now, after excludable time, and can I see the calculation?"

**CTA (1:10)**
This is general information, not legal advice for your specific case. Link in bio if your case feels like it's been sitting too long and you want help understanding where it actually stands.
