Code of ethics

The rules this reporting follows

Most readers arrive here in the middle of a criminal case. A mistake costs that reader something, so the rules are written down. Case Analytica follows the Society of Professional Journalists Code of Ethics. The rules below are the ones added to it, or applied in a specific way, because of what this site covers.

Accuracy

  • Every fact, figure and legal claim traces to a source a reader can check. Statutes link to the section text. Reporting links to the outlet that did it.
  • Before a legal claim runs, I read it against the text of the law. Citing a statute is not the same as checking it.
  • No invented numbers. Where a figure is my own estimate, the article says so in those words.
  • When a story moves after it is published, I update the article in place. A dated note at the top says what changed and why.
  • I do not pay sources for interviews or information.
  • Articles do not rely on unnamed sources for facts.
  • Articles that argue a position, rather than only report or explain, carry an Analysis label above the headline.

People in the news

  • People charged with a crime are described as charged or accused. The articles use the same “alleged” standard the courts do.
  • A person in a case is named only if a prosecutor, the police or a news outlet has already named them publicly.
  • Minors are never named or pictured, whatever the source.
  • No booking photos, ever.
  • A photo that shows a recognizable face runs only with that person’s permission, or when it is a public, credited image of the person the story is about.
  • I link to video and photos taken by other news outlets. I do not copy them onto this site.
  • The criticism here is aimed at how the system works. It does not single out the judge or prosecutor who happened to be in the room.

What this site is and is not

  • This is journalism. It explains what New York law says and how the system works. It is not legal advice, and it does not tell anyone what to do in their case.
  • I write it. I have a felony conviction and served time in New York State prison. That is stated on the about page and on every byline page, so readers can weigh it.

The paid case navigation service

  • Case Analytica also offers a paid case navigation service. It is separate from the reporting, and no grant or sponsored funds pay for it.
  • Articles do not advertise it.
  • If a person who has paid for case navigation is named, quoted or written about in an article, the article says so at the bottom.

Corrections

  • I fix errors in place. An article’s “Reflects law as of” date moves only when I recheck its claims against the source.
  • I name substantive errors on the methodology page instead of quietly patching them.
  • To report an error, email caseanalytica@gmail.com and point at the sentence.

How articles are sourced, and the corrections record, are on the methodology page. Who funds this work is on the supporters page.

Case Analytica: general information about the New York State justice system, not legal advice for your specific case. Always confirm specifics with a licensed attorney.
