## The project

Case Analytica, a fiscally sponsored project of the Alternative Newsweekly Foundation
(501(c)(3)), will document how New York's Board of Parole treats people who hold an Earned
Eligibility Certificate. The law says they "shall be granted parole release" unless the Board
makes two specific findings. They are denied anyway, and no one publishes how often.

## Why it is urgent

The urgency is today, October 13, 2026, and every week after it. Every week the Parole Board
denies release to people who hold an Earned Eligibility Certificate, and they stay in prison. The
state prison system's own Earned Eligibility Program report counts 369 denials of certificate
holders between April and September 2025, about 14 a week. (DOCCS counts Board appearances, not
individual people.) Each of those denials should rest on the two findings Correction Law § 805
requires. Nobody has published whether the written decisions state them. Every week this goes
unchecked, more people stay held on decisions no one outside the Board has read against the law.

## Who leads it

Dean Mustaphalli, who founded Case Analytica, served time in New York State prison. Inside, he
ran a transition program that prepared more than 100 men for parole hearings, and he won his own parole board
appeal. He came home in December 2023. The packets come from that work.

## Result

The written decisions, read against the statute and published with names removed on the site's
New York parole data page, and sent to the legislature's corrections committees.
