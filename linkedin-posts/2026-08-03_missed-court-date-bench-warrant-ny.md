## Post copy

Missing a court date in New York feels like the emergency. The deadline that actually matters is a different one, and almost nobody explains it before someone spends a week making decisions out of fear.

A bench warrant under CPL 530.70 isn't proof of anything — it's a tool the court uses to compel your appearance, because appearing was the condition of your release. Getting back on the calendar doesn't always require an arrest either: under CPL 530.60, some judges will restore a case without a warrant ever being executed, if your attorney reaches the court first.

What actually turns a missed date into a separate crime is the calendar. New York's bail jumping statutes only apply when you don't appear "on the required date or voluntarily within thirty days thereafter." Miss that window and the missed date can become its own charge, on top of the original case.

Ask your attorney: "Are we still inside the 30-day window, and can we get back in front of the judge before it closes?"
