## Post copy

A young man once showed me a Desk Appearance Ticket and asked what happens next. His lawyer told him to show up on the date printed on it and take whatever the DA offered.

Nobody had mentioned Project Reset. He would have qualified. He never got the chance to ask, because nobody told him the question existed.

That's the pattern I see over and over. Not a bad lawyer, usually — just a program that has to be raised by someone, before the court date arrives, and often isn't.

Project Reset is a pre-arraignment diversion program run through the district attorney's office in each of the five NYC boroughs. If someone is arrested for a low-level, non-violent misdemeanor and receives a Desk Appearance Ticket, they may be eligible to complete a short community-based program instead of ever going to court — typically two to four hours of workshops or counseling. Complete it, and the DA declines to prosecute entirely. No conviction. No trial. The arrest record is sealed.

This isn't a marginal program: participants have a 96% attendance rate once scheduled, and more than 10,000 people have avoided a criminal record through it.

Eligibility is decided case by case by the DA's office in the borough where the arrest happened — which is exactly why this has to come up early, not be assumed.

If you or someone you love just got a DAT, ask directly, the same day: "Am I eligible for Project Reset or an equivalent diversion program in this borough?"
