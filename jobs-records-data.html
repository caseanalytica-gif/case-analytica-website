Data

New York City jobs and records data.

How many jobs New York City is offering, whether the private market is growing, how many people are coming home with records, and how far Clean Slate sealing has gotten. Most of those numbers are published somewhere, never together. The last one is not published at all. This page keeps them in one place, with the source attached to every figure.

Every row was read at its source on the date it shows. The same rules govern this page as the parole data page: the figure exactly as the source states it, different measures kept in separate rows, and gaps listed rather than filled with estimates.

Why these numbers belong together

A person coming home from prison or finishing a sentence in New York City needs work, and a conviction follows them into every background check until it is sealed. Whether they find work depends on how many jobs exist, who is hiring, and whether the record is still visible. The first two are measured every month. The third, the count of convictions sealed under the Clean Slate Act, has not been published at all. Putting them side by side shows where the gap is.

The reporting behind this page is in The City Counted 15,530 Empty Jobs. Its Job Board Listed 3,821.

What counts as a source

The agency's own published data first: NYC Open Data, NY Open Data, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the court system. Then an official report, such as the Comptroller's. Then a named news outlet that obtained the figure directly. Nothing estimated, and nothing repeated between outlets without an origin.

What each field means

Every row is one figure.

  • Measure. What is being counted, in plain words.
  • Value. The figure as the source states it, or a total computed directly from the source's own rows, which the Note says.
  • Period. The date or span the figure covers, as the source defines it.
  • Population. Who or what is counted, and where.
  • Source. The organization and the dataset or document, linked.
  • Retrieved. The date the figure was read. Not the date the source was published.
  • Note. Any caveat the source attaches, and how a total was computed.

How often this is updated

Most of these sources refresh monthly or yearly. Case Analytica plans to recheck each row monthly and to ask the court system and DCJS each month for the count of sealed convictions, recording the answer or the lack of one. Each row's Retrieved date is the only promise made about it.

How to cite this

Cite the underlying source, not this page. Every row links to where the figure came from. If you need to point at the compilation itself:

Case Analytica, New York City Jobs and Records Data, caseanalytica.com/jobs-records-data.html, retrieved [date].

Known gaps

  • How many New York City residents have a felony conviction. No public dataset counts it. The supervision and release rows above are the closest published measures, and they count only people currently supervised or recently released.
  • Unemployment among people with records in New York City. We found no current city figure.
  • Hiring and openings for the city alone. The rows above are statewide.
  • Convictions sealed so far. See the last row.
