## LinkedIn

**Coming home on parole in New York? Ask in writing whether a Certificate of Relief was issued for you.**

---

Not everyone gets one. A person with more than one felony conviction is not eligible for this certificate, and DOCCS can defer it for 24 months, for example after time in disciplinary confinement in the year before release.

For the people who are eligible, DOCCS prepares the certificate before release. When the person goes home on parole, the original goes to the Bureau Chief at the parole office. The directive has no step that tells the person it exists or hands it over.

It matters because Correction Law 753(2) tells an employer weighing a conviction to consider the certificate, and it creates a presumption of rehabilitation for the offenses listed on it.

One man came home on parole in December 2023 and learned this fall that his certificate was issued on December 12, 2023. When he asked for it, his parole officer declined. The state's Certificate Review Unit confirmed it sent the certificate to the parole office, and told him it can only be sent to him after parole is over.

That is one account, not a count. The state collects monthly numbers and has not published them, so we asked DCJS for the yearly count under the Freedom of Information Law. The exact question to ask, and the order to ask it in, is in the article.

## Facebook

**If you came home from a New York prison on parole, a certificate that helps with job applications may be sitting in your parole office.**

---

Not everyone is eligible, and some are deferred for 24 months. But for people who are, DOCCS prepares a Certificate of Relief from Disabilities before release and sends the original to the parole office. Nothing in its directive tells you it exists. Under Correction Law 753(2), an employer has to consider it.

Ask your parole officer in writing: "Was a Certificate of Relief from Disabilities issued for me, and where is the original?" Keep a copy of the question and the answer. The article has the other two places to ask.
