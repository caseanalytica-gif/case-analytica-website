## LinkedIn

**In a New York mental health court, the guilty plea comes first and the treatment comes after.**

---

Finish the program and the charges are generally reduced or dismissed, New York Focus reported in August. Don't finish, and some participants are sentenced to incarceration.

Whether the option exists depends on the county. The Office of Court Administration counts 30 counties with a mental health court. About half of the state's 62 have none, and unlike drug courts, these courts aren't required by state law.

Brooklyn's court, open since 2002, has graduated 1,560 people. Its recent graduation rates have run from 73 to 87 percent, per Center for Justice Innovation data. The wait is long: a median 265 days last year from arrest to first contact with the court's clinical staff.

**Before anyone takes a mental health court plea, there's one question to ask: what happens if the program doesn't work out?**

---

In New York's mental health courts, a person usually pleads guilty to get treatment in the community. Finishing the program generally gets the charges reduced or dismissed. Not finishing can mean incarceration.

About half of New York's counties don't have one of these courts at all. In Brooklyn, which does, the median wait from arrest to first contact with the court's clinical staff was 265 days last year.
