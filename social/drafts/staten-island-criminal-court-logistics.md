Arraignment is where a judge decides whether a person goes home. In the Bronx, Manhattan and Brooklyn, that court runs until 1:00 a.m. In Queens it runs until 11:00 p.m.

On Staten Island, per the court system's own posted hours, arraignments run Monday through Friday from 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. On Saturday and Sunday they run from 9:30 a.m. to 1:00 p.m. There is no night part.

Work through what that means for an arrest at six on a Friday evening. The weekday part has closed. The weekend part opens Saturday at 9:30 and shuts at one in the afternoon. New York is supposed to bring a person before a judge without unnecessary delay, and under People ex rel. Maxian v. Brown a delay past 24 hours is presumptively unreasonable. But the 24-hour clock and the courthouse hours are two separate things, and only one of them is posted on a wall.

This is the kind of fact that never makes it into an explainer because it is nobody's beat. It is not a statute, it is not a policy debate, it is a schedule. It also decides how long a specific person sits in a cell before anyone with authority looks at their case.

The article has the address, the ferry and railway directions the court's own page leaves out, and the hours for the clerk's office, which close earlier than people expect.

If someone you love was picked up on Staten Island outside court hours, the question to ask is not when the 24 hours runs out. It is when the next arraignment part is actually sitting.

The Bronx, Manhattan and Brooklyn arraign until 1:00 a.m. Queens goes until 11:00 p.m. Staten Island stops at 5:00 p.m. on weekdays, and on weekends it only runs 9:30 a.m. to 1:00 p.m.

Arraignment is the hearing where a judge decides whether someone comes home or stays in. So the hours are not a technicality. An arrest at six on a Friday means waiting for Saturday morning, and if that Saturday part has already closed at one, waiting again.

People hear "they have to see a judge within 24 hours" and assume that settles it. The 24 hours is a legal standard. The courthouse hours are a schedule. They are not the same thing and nobody explains the difference until you are living it.

The full page has the address on Central Avenue, how to actually get there by ferry or the Staten Island Railway, and the clerk's office hours.

If this is happening to someone right now, ask their attorney or the precinct when the next arraignment part is actually sitting. That answer tells you when they can realistically come home.
