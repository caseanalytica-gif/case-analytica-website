## LinkedIn

New York's own court system says it can't seal a big share of records the way the law is written. The fix has been sitting in a committee for a year and a half.

The Clean Slate Act seals records one conviction at a time. Each conviction gets its own waiting period, its own eligibility check. On paper that sounds precise.

Most cases don't come with one conviction. One arrest, one docket, two or three counts on it. That's ordinary. New York's Unified Court System told lawmakers that the offense-by-offense structure in the statute is what's blocking the automated system from processing those dockets at all. It can't seal one count while another sits unresolved, and the law doesn't tell it what to do.

Senator Zellnor Myrie introduced S6492 to fix that on March 14, 2025. It's still in the Senate Codes Committee. The Assembly companion, A10951, wasn't introduced until April 2026 and hasn't moved either. Neither has a floor vote scheduled.

The deadline to finish sealing every eligible record in the state is November 16, 2027. Roughly fourteen months out.

The NYC Comptroller's office estimated more than half a million city residents would become eligible under Clean Slate, with $2.4 billion a year in potential wage gains once people stop getting screened out of jobs. Nearly 80 percent of that group is Black or Latinx.

Technical corrections bills are normal after big legislation. A fix the court system itself flagged as necessary sitting untouched for over a year, while the clock it's meant to protect keeps running, is worth asking your state senator about.

If your case had more than one count on it, the system New York is building to seal your record may not know what to do with you.

Clean Slate was written to seal convictions one at a time. But a single arrest often produces a single case with two or three counts attached. The state's own court technology division told lawmakers that the way the law is worded, the automated sealing system can't handle those cases at all.

There's a bill to fix it. S6492. It was introduced March 14, 2025 and has been sitting in the Senate Codes Committee ever since. The Assembly version showed up more than a year later and hasn't moved either. No floor vote on either one.

New York has until November 16, 2027 to seal every eligible record in the state.

Over half a million New York City residents are supposed to become eligible under this law. If your record has more than one count on the same docket, don't assume the waiting period alone gets you there. Check your actual status through DCJS rather than counting the calendar.
