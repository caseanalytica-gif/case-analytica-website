**In New York, a conviction blocks your vote only while you are in prison for a felony. Register by October 24.**

---

Election Law § 5-106 bars voting only while someone is incarcerated for a felony. People on probation can vote, and since May 2021, so can people on parole. People held in jail before trial, or serving time on a misdemeanor, can vote by absentee ballot.

The catch is registration. Election Law § 5-400 cancels the registration of anyone convicted of a disqualifying felony. Getting the right back on release does not put you back on the rolls. You have to register again.

The 2021 law also put a duty on the state. It has to tell people leaving prison, verbally and in writing, that their rights are restored, and hand them a registration form with help filling it out.

For November 3, the last day to register is Saturday, October 24. Early voting runs October 24 to November 1.

The article has every statute linked, the dates, and the one question to ask your county board of elections.

## Facebook

**On parole or probation in New York? You can vote on November 3. Register by October 24.**

---

New York only blocks voting while someone is in prison for a felony. If you are home, on parole, on probation, or never went to prison, you can vote. If you are in jail waiting on trial, or doing time on a misdemeanor, you can vote by absentee ballot.

One thing trips people up. If you went to prison on a felony, New York cancelled your old voter registration. Coming home does not turn it back on. You have to register again, and the deadline for November 3 is Saturday, October 24.

Check your registration before then at voterlookup.elections.ny.gov, or in New York City at vote.nyc. If nothing comes up, register that day.
