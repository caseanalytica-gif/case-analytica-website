## LinkedIn

**If your record should have been sealed and wasn't, the fix is a form. The court system hasn't released it.**

---

Suppose your conviction qualifies for sealing under the Clean Slate Act and an employer runs a background check and turns you down anyway. CPL § 160.57 has a fix for that: once a valid form is submitted, the conviction is to be sealed within thirty days.

The court system's own page says the form "will be available on our website to request this review no later than the implementation date of November 16, 2027." As of November 6, 2026, it is not out. That is 375 days away, and I found no page that gives a release date.

The piece covers what the statute promises, what the state criminal history office says an employer can currently see, and what to do now: request your own record, count your waiting period from release, and keep a dated note of any rejection.

If your record qualifies for sealing and an employer rejects you anyway, the law says a valid form gets the conviction sealed within thirty days. The court system says that form will be available "no later than" November 16, 2027, and it isn't available now.

You don't have to wait to find out where you stand. Request your own record from the state criminal history office, count your waiting period from your release date, and write down the date of any rejection. The article walks through each step.
