## LinkedIn

**The Parole Board decides from a folder. Before my interview, I was shown only part of mine.**

---

Before my own parole interview, the ORC walked me through what was going to the Board. When I saw the full folder about a week before the interview, it held far more.

This is the method I used, and the one I used to help other men prepare:

1. Build the portfolio first: your story and your release plan, sent to the SORC well ahead.
2. Ask your ORC in writing to see the final file going to the commissioners.
3. Learn every document and its date. At my review there were no copies, no pen and no paper.
4. Put the dates on the record. Ask the commissioner to confirm the dates on the sentencing-era letters, and today's date.
5. Know that the transcript is the appeal. Under 9 NYCRR 8006.2 an appeal is decided on the written record, and a personal appearance is expressly prohibited.

Executive Law 259-i tells the Board to weigh the district attorney's and the sentencing court's recommendations, so those letters belong in the folder. The record has to show how old they are, next to everything written since.

The article has a printable worksheet for each step and a 33-second animated dramatization of step 4.

The Parole Board decides from a folder. Before my own interview I was shown only part of mine. When I saw the full file, it held letters from the time of my sentencing, years old, sitting next to everything I had done since.

So I learned every document and its date, and in the interview I asked the commissioner to confirm those dates and today's date, on the record. If the Board denies release, the appeal is decided on that written record alone.
