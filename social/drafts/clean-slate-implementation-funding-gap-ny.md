## LinkedIn

Your record can qualify for sealing under Clean Slate and still sit there unsealed. Those are two different things.

New York's Clean Slate Act makes roughly 5 million criminal records eligible for automatic sealing by November 16, 2027. That's the deadline the state gave itself to build the system that does the sealing.

In budget testimony in early 2025, the agencies responsible for building it told lawmakers they weren't asking for extra money or extra staff. Chief Administrative Judge Joseph Zayas said the court system had what it needed. A month later, a DCJS spokesperson said the courts had not yet sent the department a single record to seal.

Assemblywoman Catalina Cruz, who sponsored the bill in the Assembly, said she'd push for more funding if the agencies came back and asked. As of that hearing, they hadn't.

None of that is an accusation. It's what the public record shows, and it's worth someone checking again well before 2027 rather than finding out in 2028. A record that qualifies but isn't sealed yet still costs somebody a job.

## Facebook

If you're waiting on Clean Slate to seal your record, here's something worth knowing.

The law makes about 5 million New York records eligible for automatic sealing. The state has until November 16, 2027 to build the system that actually does it. Until then, DCJS keeps producing criminal history reports with those convictions still on them, and the form for requesting a manual review isn't published until that same 2027 date.

When lawmakers held budget hearings in 2025, the agencies building the system said they didn't need more money or staff to get it done. As of that March, the court system hadn't sent DCJS a single record yet.

Meanwhile, unemployment among formerly incarcerated people runs around 27 percent, roughly five times the general rate. Every month a qualifying record stays unsealed is a month somebody is still failing background checks the law says they shouldn't be failing much longer.

The piece walks through what the testimony actually said, and what you can do in the meantime if you think your record already qualifies.
