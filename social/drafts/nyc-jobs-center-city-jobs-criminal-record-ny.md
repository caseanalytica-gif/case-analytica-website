## LinkedIn

**NYC's new jobs center drew a line down the block on day one. If you have a record, the hard part comes after the offer.**

---

The Jobs NYC Center at 2 Lafayette Street opened to walk-ins on October 5. Videos from that morning show the line stretching under the scaffolding, and one person who waited posted that staff started turning people away after noon. The city had 14,391 open positions as of August 31, while private employers had added just 3,900 jobs all year.

The city's hiring process protects an applicant with a criminal record before and after the offer. Most titles can't ask about a record before a conditional offer. The agency can only weigh felonies and unsealed misdemeanors, has to run an Article 23-A analysis in writing, and has to give the applicant time to answer it.

The catch is the fingerprint check every new city hire goes through. Clean Slate seals a misdemeanor after three years and a felony after eight, but the courts have until November 2027 to finish sealing older records. Until a record actually seals, it is an unsealed conviction the city can consider.

There's a second question nobody has answered in public: whether a conviction that has sealed still comes back on the city's check, and whether the city can use it. State law turns on whether the check is "required by" law. The civil service statute says the city "may" require it. We've asked DCAS and DCJS and will update the article with their answers.

## Facebook

**Applying for a city job with a record? Here's what happens after the offer.**

---

NYC opened a walk-in jobs center on October 5, and the line went down the block. If you have a criminal record and you're applying for a city job, here's what to know.

For most jobs, the city can't ask about your record until after it makes you an offer. Then it fingerprints every new hire. It can only weigh felonies and unsealed misdemeanors, and before it pulls an offer it has to give you its reasons in writing and time to respond. Use that time, and answer in writing.

The courts haven't finished sealing old records under Clean Slate. They have until November 2027. If your record qualifies but hasn't sealed, the city can still see it. A Certificate of Relief from Disabilities creates a legal presumption that you've been rehabilitated, and it's worth getting before you apply.

of that. Keep the posts on that footing. The "required by" question is framed as unanswered
