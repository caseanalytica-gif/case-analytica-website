## LinkedIn

**The NYPD is ending its protest unit. The charge a protester faces is still set by state law.**

---

On Nov. 16, Mayor Zohran Mamdani and Commissioner Jessica Tisch announced the NYPD will disband its Strategic Response Group. ABC7 reports a new Patrol Support Division will still make protest arrests "in rare instances, if necessary."

So we read the statutes. Disorderly conduct is a violation, not a crime. Unlawful assembly is a class B misdemeanor. Obstructing governmental administration is a class A misdemeanor. A conviction for a violation is sealed under CPL 160.55. A misdemeanor follows different rules.

There is also a number nobody added up. The city's Department of Investigation found two NYPD arrest counts for June 1, 2020: 308 and 643.

The full article has every source, the charges, the appearance ticket rule and the question to ask at arraignment.

The announcement changes who shows up at a protest. It does not change the law for a person who is arrested at one: disorderly conduct is a violation, unlawful assembly is a misdemeanor, and a violation conviction is sealed.
