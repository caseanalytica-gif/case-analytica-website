## LinkedIn

**About half of New York's 62 counties have no mental health court. The one law that helps needs two district attorneys to say yes.**

---

Thirty counties have a mental health court, according to the Office of Court Administration, and about a dozen more are building one. New York Focus reported those numbers in August.

Since December 2025, CPL 230.21 has let a defendant outside New York City ask to move the case to a mental health court in a neighboring county. The district attorneys of both counties have to consent. The statute gives no standard for when either can refuse.

That law has changed three times in under six months. A February follow-up said a case would go back to the original court if the defendant failed the program. The May budget repealed that rule in one line.

The broader fix, the Treatment Court Expansion Act, would put a diversion part in every county and let most people in without a guilty plea. It has never reached a floor vote. On September 23 the Senate struck the old bill, S4547, and refiled it the same day as S10706.

The article has the statute text and the question to ask a defense attorney early.

---

About half of New York's counties don't have a mental health court. Since December, a law lets a defendant outside New York City ask to transfer the case to one in a neighboring county. Both district attorneys have to sign off, and nobody else has to bring it up.

At the Brooklyn Mental Health Court, the median wait from arrest to first contact with the court's clinical staff was 265 days last year.

A bill to put a diversion court in every county has been around since 2019. It has never had a floor vote, and the Senate refiled it again on September 23.

The article explains what to ask the defense attorney, and when.
