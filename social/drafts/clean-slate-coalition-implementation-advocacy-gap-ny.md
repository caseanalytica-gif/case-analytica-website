## LinkedIn

**New York passed Clean Slate three years ago. At the last public count, the courts hadn't sent the state a single record to seal.**

---

It took four years and a genuinely unusual coalition to get Clean Slate signed: directly impacted organizers, the Center for Community Alternatives, Legal Aid, Legal Action Center, Brooklyn Defender Services, Community Service Society, JPMorganChase, the Business Council of New York State, labor unions, and eventually four district attorneys. Hochul signed it in November 2023. The Brennan Center published a case study on how they pulled it off.

The state then gave itself until November 16, 2027 to build the system that actually seals a record automatically. At a March 2025 budget hearing, the Division of Criminal Justice Services said the Office of Court Administration had not yet sent it a single record.

Now look at what those same organizations are publicly campaigning on in 2026. CCA's campaign page points at the Earned Time Act and the Second Look Act. The Fortune Society's 2026 priorities list is long and serious — closing Rikers, protecting Raise the Age, the reentry stipend bill, Fair and Timely Parole, Elder Parole. Clean Slate isn't on it, not as a bill and not as a deadline to watch. In 2023 it was named specifically.

To be fair about what that does and doesn't show: a bill missing from a public priorities list isn't proof nobody's working on it. Plenty of implementation work happens in technical meetings that never generate a press release. The narrower, fairer claim is that none of these groups are publicly campaigning on the 2027 deadline the way they campaigned on the bill.

The state's own reporting has gone quiet too. Chief Administrative Judge Zayas gave several pages of budget testimony in February 2026 covering staffing, Family Court backlogs, e-filing. The largest records project the court system is currently required to build didn't come up once.

This isn't an accusation, it's an appeal. The people who won Clean Slate are the same people with the standing in Albany to make sure 2027 doesn't quietly become 2028. Every year a qualifying record sits unsealed is a year it can still cost someone a job or a lease.

## Facebook

**New York's Clean Slate Act was supposed to seal old records automatically. Almost three years in, the count is still zero.**

---

New York's Clean Slate Act was signed in November 2023. It's supposed to seal old conviction records automatically. As of the most recent public reporting, the courts hadn't sent the state a single record to seal yet.

The state has until November 16, 2027 to build the system that does it. That deadline is the whole ballgame for a lot of people waiting on a record.

Here's the part worth paying attention to. The coalition that spent four years winning that bill was huge — reentry organizations, legal services, labor unions, business groups, four DAs. Look at what those same groups are publicly pushing in 2026 and Clean Slate implementation barely comes up. Their public energy has moved to other bills, most of them good bills.

That's not an accusation that nobody's working on it. A lot of implementation work happens quietly. But there's a difference between quiet technical meetings and the kind of loud public pressure that got the law passed in the first place, and right now the second one isn't there.

If you think your record should already qualify, don't wait passively for 2027. You can request a record review through DCJS, and depending on your situation a Certificate of Relief or the older CPL 160.59 sealing motion may remove the same barriers without waiting on the automatic system.
