The Clean Slate Fix for a Missed Sealing Is a Form. The Form Doesn't Exist Yet.

By Dean Mustaphalli·2026-11-06

Reflects law as of November 6, 2026

Share X Facebook LinkedIn Republish

This article is free, and always will be. If it helped you or someone you love, Case Analytica runs on reader support.

♥ Chip In

Suppose your record qualifies for sealing under the Clean Slate Act, and an employer runs a background check and turns you down anyway. The statute has a fix for that. The fix does not exist yet.

The fix is a form. The court system's own record sealing page says the law lets people "ask for a review of their convictions if they think they should have been sealed but were not," and that the form "will be available on our website to request this review no later than the implementation date of November 16, 2027." It adds that the form is not available yet. The court system's FAQ says the same: "New York State has until November 16, 2027, to implement Clean Slate legislation" and "Once the system is ready, the form will be made available."

What the statute promises

Criminal Procedure Law § 160.57 sets the waiting periods: three years from release for a misdemeanor and eight years for a felony. Subdivision 6 requires the court system to "make diligent efforts to promptly seal all conviction records eligible for sealing" and to finish "no later than three years after such effective date." The law took effect November 16, 2024. Three years ends November 16, 2027.

Subdivision 1(e) is the safety valve. Where the required sealing "has not taken place" and a defendant or their attorney submits a valid form, the conviction "shall be sealed as set forth in this subdivision within thirty days of the receipt of such form." Thirty days. That is a real promise. A form that does not exist cannot trigger it.

The math on the gap

Today is November 6, 2026. The deadline is 375 days away. Until the system is ready, a person whose record should already be sealed has no way to ask for sealing, and a person whose record is eligible but not yet processed has nothing to file. The state court system has said it will release the form "no later than" the deadline, which means the date could be tomorrow or the last week of the three years. No page I found gives a date.

Meanwhile the state's own criminal history office says what an employer can see. The DCJS record review page states that "DCJS cannot seal records until receiving an order from the court to do so," and that until then DCJS review results "will still show convictions that will later be sealed." Records of people convicted of sex crimes and non-drug Class A felonies "will not be sealed."

What a missed sealing costs

The Prison Policy Initiative's Out of Prison & Out of Work reports that formerly incarcerated people are unemployed at a rate of over 27 percent, and says employment "reduces the likelihood that they return to prison." That is a national figure from a 2018 report, not a New York count. Still, read it next to the court system's page. A person who qualifies today and is rejected today is being treated as a person with a record, because on paper the record is still there. Every month of that is a month of blocked applications. Each one moves a person further from the stable job the law was written to open.

General information, not legal advice

Eligibility depends on the specific conviction, the dates of release, and any later convictions. Whether a particular record has been sealed, and whether the form will apply to your situation once released, can only be confirmed by a licensed attorney reviewing your actual record.

What to do now

Do not wait for the form to find out where you stand. Request your own record from DCJS. The record review page says fingerprints are required, results come by mail, and the fee in New York was $17.50 as of February 2, 2026, with a fee waiver application available if you cannot pay. Count your waiting period from your release date. If you were rejected for a job, write down the date and the employer. When the form is released, that file is your submission. Then check back on the court system's sealing page instead of assuming someone will tell you.

For how the three-year clock works and who qualifies, read the Clean Slate Act explainer. For the money behind the build, see New York says it doesn't need more money to seal your record. For the bill meant to fix a known sealing bug, see S6492.

Sources: CPL § 160.57 · NY Courts, record sealing · NY Courts FAQ · DCJS · Prison Policy Initiative

The exact question to ask

Ask your attorney or a legal services provider: "Does my conviction meet the waiting period under CPL 160.57, and has it been sealed yet?" Then ask DCJS for your own record. If it shows a conviction that should be sealed, save it with the date. Do not wait to be told the form is out.
