Waiting on Clean Slate? NYC Pays $30 an Hour to Shovel Snow, and the Sign-Up Never Asked About a Record

By Dean Mustaphalli·2026-10-13

Reflects law as of October 13, 2026

Share X Facebook LinkedIn Republish

This article is free, and always will be. If it helped you or someone you love, Case Analytica runs on reader support.

♥ Chip In

On Saturday, October 10, Case Analytica went to the Department of Sanitation's emergency snow shoveler sign-up at PS 40 in Jamaica, Queens. The paperwork was a federal W-4, a New York State withholding form, proof of a Social Security number and proof of address. There was no job application. Nobody asked about arrests or convictions, and nobody was fingerprinted.

That matters for anyone still waiting on Clean Slate to seal their record.

If you're waiting on Clean Slate

Under CPL 160.57, New York's Clean Slate Act makes a misdemeanor conviction eligible to seal three years after sentencing or release from incarceration, and a felony after eight. Sex offenses and Class A felonies other than drug offenses never seal. Nothing seals while you are on probation or parole for that conviction, or while a new charge is pending in New York.

Eligible and sealed are not the same thing yet. The court system's Clean Slate page says it has until November 16, 2027 to seal every eligible conviction. Until then, a conviction that qualifies can still show up on a background check.

For most city jobs, that check comes after the offer, when the city runs the new hire's fingerprints, as we reported last week. At the snow shoveler sign-up we attended, there was no check for a record to show up on.

What it pays

The city raised the rate this year. Shovelers earn $30 an hour, rising to $45 after the first 40 hours in a week, up from $19.14 and $28.71, according to the Mayor's Office announcement on September 15. More than 7,800 New Yorkers shoveled during heavy snowfalls last winter, the same announcement says.

It is not a steady job. DSNY says it recruits "temporary, per diem (by day) shovelers as needed." DSNY assigns garages based on borough needs and the top three locations you pick when you register. At the Queens sign-up, that happened at the table: you leave with three garages, then wait for the first snow and a call. DSNY says it contacts shovelers by email or phone. You are paid for the days you work. DSNY says to bring your own weather-resistant clothing.

Who can sign up

DSNY lists three requirements:

  • You are at least 18.
  • You are legally authorized to work in the United States.
  • You can do heavy physical labor outdoors in winter weather.

That is the whole list. DSNY's page does not mention a background check or criminal records, and neither does the Mayor's Office announcement.

What to bring

  • A phone number and an email address.
  • Your original Social Security card. DSNY says no photocopies.
  • Original, unexpired documents from the I-9 list, the federal list of IDs that prove you can work here.
  • A completed W-4 and New York State IT-2104. DSNY's page calls these optional. The Mayor's Office and the Jobs NYC event page list them as required. Fill them out before you go. Both were part of the sign-up we attended.
  • Proof of address. DSNY's page doesn't list it. Staff asked for it at the Queens sign-up.

You no longer need to bring photos. The Mayor's Office says they are taken at the sign-up this year.

Register before November 30

DSNY says anyone who finishes registering before November 30 gets a paid four-hour orientation in December and "fast-tracked processing to receive a paycheck within 2 weeks of working." After October 31, you can email ShovelSnow@dsny.nyc.gov to check the status of your registration.

Where to sign up

DSNY's list as of October 13:

  • Thursday, Oct. 15, noon to 4 p.m. DSNY Graffiti Headquarters, 1628 Bathgate Avenue, Bronx
  • Saturday, Oct. 17, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. Susan E. Wagner High School, 1200 Manor Road, Staten Island
  • Monday, Oct. 19, noon to 4 p.m. DSNY Headquarters, 125 Worth Street, 2nd floor auditorium, Manhattan
  • Saturday, Oct. 24, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m. PS 197 Kings Highway Academy, 1599 East 22nd Street, Brooklyn

Staff at the Queens sign-up said the events will keep moving through all five boroughs over the next few weeks. DSNY says more dates are coming. Check its page before you go.

What we didn't see

We saw the sign-up. We did not see what happens after it. DSNY's page doesn't say whether there is any check before a first shift. If you are asked about your record after signing up, tell us.

The exact question to ask

Ask at the registration table, before you leave: "Which three garages am I assigned to, and will you call me or email me when it snows?" Write the three garages down, and make sure the phone number and email you gave them are ones you check. When the call comes, DSNY says you have to show your registration confirmation email at the garage, so keep it on your phone. "We'll reach out" doesn't tell you how.
