How Much Does a Criminal Defense Lawyer Actually Cost in New York?

Nobody posts a price list for a criminal defense attorney, and calling around for quotes while you're also dealing with an active case is its own kind of exhausting. Here's what actually drives the number, so a first consultation feels less like a black box.

What the ranges generally look like

Nobody publishes authoritative numbers for this, and anyone who tells you they have them is guessing too. What follows is my own estimate, from the fee conversations I have watched people go through, not a survey and not a quote. Treat it as orientation for a first phone call.

Misdemeanor retainers tend to land somewhere around $2,500 to $7,500. Felony retainers tend to start around $5,000 to $10,000 and climb well past $50,000 once a charge is serious and complex. Attorneys who bill hourly instead of taking a flat fee or a retainer are usually somewhere between $250 and $500 an hour, and Manhattan runs higher than the outer boroughs and higher again than most upstate counties. All of it moves with the county, the complexity, and how long the attorney has been doing this. Your actual number can sit outside every range on this page and still be reasonable.

Get it in writing, and ask this directly

"Is this a flat fee for the whole case, or does it only cover work up to a certain point, like before trial?" A huge share of sticker shock in criminal defense comes from a retainer that covered pretrial work only, with a separate, much larger fee kicking in if the case goes to trial. Ask this before signing anything, not after.

What actually moves the price

Four things drive cost more than anything else: the severity of the charge, whether the case is likely to go to trial versus resolve in a plea, how much investigation or expert testimony the defense actually needs, and the attorney's own experience and caseload. A trial adds cost fast, since it means witness prep, motions, and days or weeks of a lawyer's calendar that a plea negotiation doesn't require.

If a full-time private attorney isn't realistic

A public defender is a real, licensed attorney at no cost if you qualify financially, and qualifying is more common than people assume; see our breakdown of what a public defender's caseload actually looks like for the honest tradeoff involved. For people who have some counsel already, public or private, but need more time than a single overloaded schedule allows, case navigation exists as a lower-cost complement: someone reviewing documents with you in plain language and helping you prepare before each hearing, without trying to replace the attorney of record.

If the funds meant to pay a retainer were themselves seized during the arrest or an investigation, that's a separate problem with its own fix. See how civil asset forfeiture works under CPLR Article 13-A for the hardship motion that can release frozen funds specifically to pay for a defense.

General information, not legal advice

Actual fees vary case by case and firm by firm. Always get a specific, written fee agreement before retaining any attorney, and ask exactly what it does and does not cover.
