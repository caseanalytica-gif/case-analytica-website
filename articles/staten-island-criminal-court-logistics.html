Staten Island Criminal Court Has No Night Arraignments. Here Is What That Means

By Dean Mustaphalli·2026-09-19

Reflects law as of September 19, 2026

The thing people get wrong about Staten Island

Staten Island has no night arraignment part. Arraignments run Monday through Friday until 5:00 p.m., and on weekends only from 9:30 a.m. to 1:00 p.m. Every other borough runs until 11 p.m. or 1 a.m. An arrest on a Friday evening here is not getting arraigned that night.

Why this matters more than the address

In the Bronx, Manhattan and Brooklyn, arraignments run until 1:00 a.m. In Queens they run until 11:00 p.m. On Staten Island, per the court system's own listing, they stop at 5:00 p.m. on weekdays and 1:00 p.m. on weekends.

That is not a scheduling footnote. Arraignment is where a judge decides whether someone goes home. Somebody arrested at six on a Friday evening in Staten Island is held until the weekend part opens, and if the weekend part has already closed at one in the afternoon, they wait again. New York is supposed to bring someone before a judge without unnecessary delay, and a delay past 24 hours is presumptively unreasonable, but the clock and the courthouse hours are two different things.

Where it is

The Richmond County Criminal Court building is at 26 Central Avenue, in St. George.

Getting there

The court system's page does not list transit directions for Staten Island. In practice:

  • Ferry: the courthouse is roughly a ten minute walk from the St. George Ferry Terminal. For most people coming from another borough this is the route.
  • Staten Island Railway: to St. George, the northern terminus, which shares the terminal with the ferry.
  • Bus: the S52, S76, S78, S42 and S46 all serve the area.
  • There is no subway to Staten Island. Budget the ferry crossing, which runs about 25 minutes, plus the wait, on top of everything else.

The hours that matter

  • Arraignments: Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m. Saturday and Sunday, 9:30 a.m. to 1:00 p.m.
  • Desk Appearance Tickets, all other court parts, and the clerk's offices: Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., closed holidays.
  • General information: (718) 675-8558.

The exact question to ask

If someone was arrested on Staten Island in the evening or over a weekend, ask their attorney or the precinct: "When is the next arraignment part actually sitting?" Not when the 24 hours runs out. When the part opens. Those are different questions here in a way they are not in the other four boroughs, and the answer tells you when someone can realistically come home.

This page is about logistics only. Nothing here is advice about a specific case.
