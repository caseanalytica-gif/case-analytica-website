New York Counts Every Person in Its Jails Each Morning. I Cannot Find a Count of the Records It Has Sealed.

By Case Analytica·2026-11-02

Reflects law and data as of November 2, 2026

New York City counted 6,665 people in its jails today. State law sets November 16, 2027 as the deadline to seal every eligible old conviction, and I cannot find a published count of how many it has sealed so far. One of those numbers arrives every day. The other has not arrived at all.

That gap is why Case Analytica now keeps a public data hub. The jail, prison and parole figures on it come straight from agency files, and I read them again each day. The sealing count is the number the hub does not have. On October 31 I sent records requests to the state Division of Criminal Justice Services (DCJS) and to the court system asking for it. This morning DCJS confirmed it received mine.

The request can come back two ways. A number tells readers how far along sealing is with 379 days left. A written statement that no record exists tells them nobody is tracking it in a form that can be released. I will publish whichever comes back, and I will add the number to the data hub the day it arrives.

What gets counted every day

The Department of Correction posts a daily file of who is in its custody. On November 2 it showed 6,665 people: 6,131 men, 522 women and 12 not recorded. The file holds one day only. If nobody saves each morning's copy, yesterday disappears, so the daily history on the hub starts on October 31, the first day I saved it. One more detail from the agency's own notes: the file leaves out sealed cases.

The state prison system publishes on a slower schedule. On March 31, 2026, 33,884 people were in its custody, down from 62,597 on March 31, 2008. It admitted 10,125 people in 2025, down from 26,063 in 2008. In 2025 the Board of Parole approved 2,409 of its 5,672 first-interview decisions, or 42.5 percent. In 2008 the figure was 22.3 percent. Those are three different measures, and I do not add them together.

Every one of those numbers has a date, an agency and a link.

What the jail count says about Rikers

The city's own law requires Rikers Island to close by August 31, 2027. That is 302 days from today. City officials acknowledged in June 2026 that they would not make it, according to Straight Arrow News, which reported the count at 6,541 in late August. The same report says the four replacement jails will hold 4,160 people combined. Brooklyn is expected to finish in 2029, the Bronx and Queens may open by 2031, and Manhattan is expected to finish in 2032.

Take today's 6,665 and subtract those 4,160 beds. The difference is 2,505 people. That subtraction is mine, not the report's, and it assumes the count stays where it is. It shows the distance between the plan and the people. Anyone can check it, because the count behind it is public and dated.

What nobody has counted yet

The Clean Slate Act, CPL § 160.57, seals misdemeanor convictions three years after release and felony convictions eight years after release. The court system must finish sealing the convictions that were already eligible no later than three years after the law took effect. The law took effect November 16, 2024, so the deadline is November 16, 2027, which is 379 days away. The statute has the court system tell DCJS each time it seals a conviction. So two agencies hold the information a count would need.

Two clocks

  1. Nov 2, 2026 · today

    6,665 people in city jails. No sealing count found.

  2. Aug 31, 2027

    Legal closure date for Rikers. 302 days.

  3. Nov 16, 2027

    Deadline to seal eligible pre-existing convictions. 379 days.

I have not found a count. The court system's sealing pages that I used for my November 1 article do not give one. Neither does the DCJS record review page, which says DCJS cannot seal records until it receives a court order. I also found no news report with a current figure. The last figure I found is more than 19 months old: in March 2025, a DCJS spokesperson told Spectrum News the court system had not yet sent the department any records to seal. I emailed the DCJS public information office on October 6 and asked whether it has a count. It has not replied.

This is not a finding that the count does not exist. It may sit in an internal report. The request is how I find out.

What I asked for, and when I will know

DCJS logged my request on November 2 as R000566-110226 and gave itself about 20 business days to answer, which lands around December 3. I asked for any report that states how many convictions have been sealed, any record of how many were identified as eligible, and any status update DCJS exchanged with the court system limited to those counts. If no such record exists, I asked DCJS to say so in writing. I sent the court system a separate request on October 31 for its own administrative records. It has not acknowledged it yet, and the law gives agencies five business days to do that, so it is not late.
