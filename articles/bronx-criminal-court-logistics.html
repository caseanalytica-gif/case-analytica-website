Bronx Criminal Court: Which Building, Which Train, What to Expect

By Dean Mustaphalli·2026-08-20

Read this part first

"Bronx Criminal Court" is two different buildings, a block and a half apart, and both go by that name. Before you travel, call (718) 618-2400 for an arraignment or (718) 618-2460 for any other part and confirm which address you need. Going to the wrong one costs you an hour you may not have.

Most of what makes a court date go badly has nothing to do with the case. People miss calendar calls because they went to the wrong building, got stuck in a security line, or budgeted for a walk that turned out to be longer than it looked. This page is only about that: where the building is, how to get in, and how to find the room. Nothing here is advice about your case.

The two buildings

New York City Criminal Court in the Bronx operates out of both of these, and the court system's own listing gives both addresses under the same name:

  • 215 East 161st Street, between Sherman and Sheridan Avenues
  • 265 East 161st Street, between Morris and Sherman Avenues

The 265 building also houses Bronx Supreme Court, Criminal Term, which is where felony cases go after indictment. So the same address can be listed under two different courts depending on which page you land on, which is exactly why you should confirm by phone rather than guess from a search result. That includes this page.

Getting there

  • Subway: the D and 4 both run to 161 St–Yankee Stadium at all hours. The B also stops there but runs on a limited weekday schedule, so if your date is on a weekend or late at night, plan on the D or the 4.
  • Bus: the BX6 and BX13 stop at East 161st Street and Sheridan Avenue. The BX1 stops at East 161st Street and Grand Concourse. If stairs are a problem, the bus puts you closer than the train does.
  • The walk from the train is longer than it sounds. The station is at 161st and River Avenue. The courthouses are several blocks east of that, and it is uphill. If you are moving slowly, bringing children, or not feeling well, add time for it.
  • Driving: street parking around 161st is metered and heavily restricted. The paid garages near the stadium and along the Grand Concourse are the realistic option. Budget for the cost.

Arraignment hours

If someone was arrested in the last day or so and has not seen a judge yet, that is an arraignment. In the Bronx, arraignments run Monday through Sunday, 9:00 a.m. to 1:00 a.m., holidays included. Desk Appearance Tickets run the same hours except holidays.

Every other court part and clerk's office runs Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., closed holidays. So a case that is past arraignment is a business-hours matter, and a case that is not can put you in that building at eleven at night.

There is no way to know in advance exactly when a case will be called. People wait a long time. Bring a phone charger, and bring something for anyone you have with you.

General information, not legal advice

Addresses, phone numbers, and hours here come from the New York State Unified Court System's public listings as of August 2026. Court schedules and part assignments change. Call before you travel, and confirm anything about your specific case with a licensed attorney.

Getting through security

Arrive at least 45 minutes before your appearance time. The line backs up hard around 9:00 a.m., and being in the building is not the same as being in the room.

Leave these at home. They will be taken at the magnetometer and you will not get them back quickly, if at all:

  • Knives of any size, scissors, tools, box cutters
  • Pepper spray or any defensive spray
  • Cameras and recording devices

Phones are generally allowed in, but recording inside a courtroom is not. Assume you will be asked to silence or surrender it in the part.

Finding the right room

Once you are through the lobby, find the directory screens and look up the last name. That gives you the part and the floor. If the name does not appear, go to the clerk's office rather than guessing, because a case can be moved.

If you are there for an arraignment, ask specifically where the arraignment part is sitting that day. It is not always where the directory sends everyone else.

Before you leave the building

Write down the next court date before you walk out, and confirm the part it is in. Missing the next one is how a case gets much worse, and a missed date and an active bench warrant are not the same emergency, but neither is something you want to find out about late.

If bail was set and it seems like an amount the family can cover but nobody has been released, that can be a separate mechanism entirely. See how a bail source hearing works. And if this is an arraignment, it is worth knowing before the next appearance that diversion and restorative justice options exist and generally have to be raised by someone.
