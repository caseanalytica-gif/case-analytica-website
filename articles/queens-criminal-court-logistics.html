Queens Criminal Court: Getting There, Getting In, and the 11 p.m. Cutoff

By Dean Mustaphalli·2026-08-20

The thing people get wrong about Queens

Queens arraignments stop at 11:00 p.m., not 1:00 a.m. The Bronx runs to 1:00 a.m. and people assume every borough is the same. If someone was arrested Tuesday evening and is not arraigned by eleven, they are held overnight and produced the next day.

This page covers the logistics of the Queens courthouse and nothing else: where it is, how to get in, when things actually happen. None of it is advice about a specific case.

Where it is

Queens Criminal Court and the Summons Part are at 125-01 Queens Boulevard in Kew Gardens, near Hoover Avenue and 82nd Avenue.

  • Subway: the E or F to Union Turnpike. Both run at all hours.
  • Bus: the Q60, Q37, Q74, and Q46 all stop close to the courthouse.
  • Driving: metered parking along Queens Boulevard is limited and turns over slowly. There is paid public parking near the complex, but on a busy morning it fills. If you can take the train, take the train.

The courthouse sits in a complex with other court buildings around it. Look for signage for Criminal Court specifically rather than following the first entrance you see.

The hours that matter

  • Arraignments: Monday through Sunday, 9:00 a.m. to 11:00 p.m.
  • Desk Appearance Tickets: Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., closed holidays
  • Clerk's Office: Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., closed holidays

Note how early the DAT and clerk windows close compared to the courthouse generally. If you need something from the clerk, a late-afternoon arrival is a wasted trip. Clerk's office general information is (718) 298-0792; the Summons Clerk is 718-298-0700.

General information, not legal advice

Addresses, phone numbers, and hours here come from the New York State Unified Court System's public listings as of August 2026. Schedules and part assignments change. Call before you travel, and confirm anything about your specific case with a licensed attorney.

Getting through security

Lines form along the outside of the building before the doors open, and the 9:00 a.m. crowd is the worst of the day. Give yourself 45 minutes between arriving and needing to be in a room.

Knives, scissors, tools, pepper spray, cameras, and recording equipment will be taken at the magnetometer. Leave them at home rather than trying to check them.

Finding the right part

Check the electronic displays in the main entry corridor for the name and the part assignment. If the name is not listed, ask at the clerk's window instead of walking floors. Cases get moved, and the boards are not always current.

The question worth asking before the next date

This is the one thing on this page that is not purely logistics, and it is here because it is the thing most people never hear in time.

New York has diversion programs, treatment-based alternatives, and problem-solving court parts that can resolve some cases without a conviction, and they are generally not offered automatically. Whether any of them applies depends on the charge, the history, and what the particular county runs, which is not something a web page can tell you. What you can do is ask your attorney directly, before a plea is entered, whether any alternative track is available in this case, and if not, why not.
